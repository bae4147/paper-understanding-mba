📄 Linking Ethical Leadership to Employee Burnout, Workplace Deviance and Performance: Testing the Mediating Roles of Trust in Leader and Surface Acting
Mo (2015)
💡 실천 전략 (Strategies)
One clear takeaway is that ethical leadership has to be visible. It cannot just be assumed. In real estate, that means being upfront about expectations, commissions, and how decisions are made. Something as simple as explaining why certain decisions are made can go a long way in building trust.

Another takeaway is creating an environment where people do not feel like they have to put on an act. Real estate can be stressful, and a lot of agents feel pressure to always look confident even when things are not going well. Encouraging open conversations about challenges and mistakes could help reduce stress and keep people engaged.

Lastly, the article shows that trust connects leadership behavior to performance. In practice, this means paying attention to how people respond over time. If leadership is fair and consistent, agents are more likely to stay engaged, communicate better, and perform at a higher level. These are simple things that do not cost money but can make a real difference.
🔄 맥락 전환 (Context Translation)

Those managers had control over things like schedules, evaluations, and job security. My situation in real estate is different. Even though the setup is different, the main takeaway still applies. In real estate, trust may actually matter more because leaders cannot force behavior. If agents do not trust leadership, they simply disengage, stop following guidance, or leave the brokerage. Ethical leadership in this environment shows up through being honest, fair, and consistent. While burnout or bad behavior may look different in real estate, like cutting corners or checking out mentally, the idea that trust in leadership affects how people perform still holds true. So the context is different, but the findings still translate well.
